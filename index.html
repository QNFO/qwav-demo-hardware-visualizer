What you're seeing

Each atom is a vertex of a tree. Parent–child pairs are coupled by Rydberg blockade gates — a two-qubit gate between the atom and its tree-neighbor. The tree is the same Bruhat-Tits structure from Artifacts A1 and A3, now made physical.

Hover any atom to read its coordinates; toggle the gate layer to see the edge set.

The tree-to-hardware mapping

A depth-d, branching-p tree has (pd+1−1)/(p−1) vertices. For p=3, d=3: 1+3+9+27 = 40 — the 40-atom layout.

Rydberg blockade couples atoms within the Rydberg radius, which is the natural physical realization of a tree edge: only adjacent vertices are coupled. Errors are confined to subtrees (see A1).

Why 4K matters

Neutral-atom arrays operate at ~4 K (or lower), unlike dilution-fridge superconducting qubits at ~15 mK. The tree layout is chosen so Rydberg gate pairs share a parent vertex — minimizing long-range interactions and keeping the ultrametric (hierarchical) error structure intact.

This is an interactive schematic, not a fabrication of measured hardware results.†
